Where it stands

Board A is placed, captured and routed, and every connection is made. 136 parts, 87 nets, 425 pin connections; 1361 track segments and 278 vias. DRC finds nothing, ERC finds nothing, nothing is unconnected, no right-angle corners survive, and KiCad's own exported netlist matches the one the repo intended — net by net, connection by connection. The whole board comes out of tools/gen_boards.py, so the drawings, the floorplan and the netlist cannot drift from the numbers the spec quotes.

Re-routed 2026-09-22 with four of the simulation's changes, and five open questions answered: XT30 for the bus, a reset brakes, SimpleFOC, VMOT down to 12 V, and no enclosure fins — 20 A is a burst to be measured on the prototype, not a continuous rating. On the board: every barrel under the low-side drains now reaches the switch-node pour on B.Cu (four of sixteen did), the high sides have twenty barrels rather than sixteen, each cell carries an unfitted RC snubber, the LIN pulls go to GND, and the magnet is Ø6. The simulation's conduction solver, re-run on the routed board, has the busiest low-side barrel at 2.82 A rms (was 5.16) and the busiest high-side one at 2.10 (was 2.42): better, and still over the 2 A guideline at a continuous 20 A; under it below about 14 A RMS.

The simulation — ten phases, fourteen questions, twenty-four findings (the report) — still describes the board before those changes; sim/results/ was not re-run into place. Its other two corrections to the design's arithmetic are not applied yet: the commutation loop is about 2 nH where geometry.commutation_loop() says 0.39, and the DC-link ceramics are worth about half their marking at 60 V.

What is not done: nobody has looked at board A's routing by eye, and board B is still an outline — now with three things waiting for it: the gate rail's enable held on through a reset, and the polymer-hybrid bulk. (USB-PD and a pack on the XT30 are not used together, so the PD front end stays as specified.)

Open decisions — waiting on a person

  1. How hard does the EG2103's output stage actually drive? The only obtainable document gives IO+/IO− with no test condition, and the whole edge hangs off it: 62.6 V of Vds with no ring and no snubber at the datasheet reading, 73.9 V and a 145 MHz ring at a strong output stage. It decides Q2, Q3, Q4 and Q12 between them. Get the real datasheet, or measure the gate edge into a known capacitance on a built board.
  2. Which motor? Sets the air gap, the standoffs, and whether the 25 × 19 mm pattern bolts to anything. Deliberately deferred — one constant in geometry.py moves the whole stack.
  3. What happens on a decel? At the design-point braking torque the bus reaches the TVS breakdown in 0.26 ms and leaves about 2 J on a part that survives a few. There is no brake chopper; the firmware's 63–66 V fold-back reacts in one 50 µs period. A brake resistor, an active clamp, much more bulk, or a documented deceleration limit. (A reset now brakes, which does not pump the bus; a controlled decel still does.)
  4. How is the motor frame bonded? Nothing on this board bonds it — the four motor mounts are NPTH — so it follows the switch node to 71 V peak against a 10 V criterion, and a long bonding wire makes it worse, not better. The sweep's best option is a 4.7 nF Y capacitor at 2 V of frame swing. Measure the motor's winding-to-frame capacitance first; the table moves with it.
  5. E-stop loop: keep it?
  6. Hardware overcurrent trip: populate, DNP, or omit.
  7. Interconnect — header, or brass standoffs as the power path.

Full text in §11 of the spec; 1, 3 and 4 come out of the simulation's own open findings. Decided 2026-09-22 and taken off this list: XT30 for the bus, a reset brakes, SimpleFOC, VMOT down to 12 V, no enclosure fins, and USB-PD as a replacement for a pack rather than alongside one.

How it got here — placement, capture, routing

The first routed board made 69 of 170 connections and blamed the driver ring. Measuring it said otherwise: nearly every open connection ended at the RP2350, which had no room to fan out. The second pass rebuilt the CPU wedge around a clear band for a four-row escape, turned the FETs so their gates face the driver row, rearranged the sense row for arcs, and took the regulator cluster from Raspberry Pi's reference design. Then the connections freerouting does badly — the QFN escape, the gates, the sense nets — were made as copper by a tool before the router ran. The last three connections turned out to be arithmetic rather than routing: the escape via rows were 0.71 mm apart and a track passing between two of them needs 0.91, so there was no radial lane out of the via field at all. Moving the outermost row out 0.2 mm bought one, and freerouting finished the board in its first round. §10 and §10b of the spec have the detail.

The phase cells are 68°, not the 60 the floorplan sweep promised. Routing found that the channel interior to the screws can only hold parts whose nets are ground or the phase output, so five per cell came back in and the cells had to pay for them. It costs the even bolt circle and buys 29 mm² of heatsink land.

The three phase cells are adjacent rather than alternating with the utility wedges — the RP2350A sits in the middle of the quiet side instead of between two switching cells, and the three motor leads leave together.

Three things unblocked the specification itself. The drive is rated 20 A RMS per phase, continuous, which settles the thermal question by paying for it. The EG2103 datasheet was read rather than argued about. And the motor is set aside — every dimension that depends on it is derived from one constant in geometry.py, so naming the motor later moves the whole stack at once.

Committing to 20 A found two real errors in draft 0.2. Capturing the schematic found seven more — the RP2350A had no core-buck inductor, the bus divider would have put 5.5 V on a 3.3 V ADC pin, and the MOSFET's symbol and footprint numbered their pins differently, which would have wired every FET backwards. That is what capture is for.

The two decisions worth arguing about
The 12 V gate rail is the whole thermal budget. The sister board drives 10 V-rated MOSFETs from a 5.04 V rail, which its own review costs at ~2 W per bridge of switching loss plus ~15% on RDS(on) (its F-41). It cannot easily fix this, because thirteen 5.5 V-max analog switches sit on that same rail. This board has no such rail and no such constraint. Taking the 12 V rail — and with it a driver that wants one — moves board A from ~18 W to 10.7 W at 20 A RMS. It also shapes the USB-PD behaviour: the gate rail's buck cannot boost, so before a PD contract is negotiated VMOT sits at 5 V, the logic and encoder are up and the motor physically cannot energise. Ask for 20 V and it runs.
20 A continuous is a rating the enclosure grants, not one the board has. Board A is sealed between the motor and board B; neither face sees moving air. Bare, the stack is about 15 K/W to ambient, and 10.7 W through that puts the junctions at 214 °C. The design is the machined aluminium ring: it clamps 279 mm² of mask-free copper across the three phase cells, bolts through all six perimeter M2.5 — now plated, padded on every layer, and doubling as the ground bond — and the whole board is laid out around it, with every outward-face part stopping at R 29.2. What is left is the outside of that ring: ≥ 100 cm² of external surface for ≤ 6.7 K/W. A plain Ø65 × 35 mm barrel is 71 cm² and misses; the same barrel with sixteen 6 mm fins is ~138 cm² and lands at 105 °C junction. The fins are the rating.
“20 A” meant two different things, and the sense chain lost — twice. The thermal budget was written at 20 A RMS per phase; the sense chain was designed for ±20.6 A instantaneous, to reproduce the sister board's 80 mV/A so its firmware constants would carry across. Those differ by √2 — the chain would have clipped on every cycle above 14.6 A RMS, and you cannot close a current loop on a clipped measurement. The first correction, 1.0 mΩ for ±32 A, was sized against the amplifier's typical output swing; the datasheet's worst case turns that into ±29 A, which is 2.5% over the peak and not margin. The shunt is now 0.8 mΩ (2 × 1.6 mΩ), 40 mV/A, ±36.2 A worst case — 28% clear. And the part number was wrong too: the 50 V/V grade of INA241 is A3, not A2. Consolation: desolder one of each pair and the precision mode lands on exactly 80 mV/A, the sister board's scaling, so its firmware constants come back after all.
How the hardware is generated

Two KiCad 9 projects, both DRC- and ERC-clean, emitted from tools/geometry.py, tools/placement.py and tools/schematic.py by tools/gen_boards.py — so the outlines, the mount patterns, the thermal land, every part position and every net come from the same numbers the spec quotes. Board A carries 136 parts and 87 nets; board B is still an outline. The three tools are checked against each other before anything is written: a part cannot be on the board and not in the netlist, or the other way round, and their footprints have to agree.

The schematic is generated and drawn with labels rather than wires — an honest description of a machine-laid-out sheet. What it is for is a netlist that cannot drift from the placement, and KiCad's own export confirms it net by net.

OriginBoard centre = the shaft axis = the MT6701 sensing centre, on both boards. Grid and drill origins set there too.
SymbolsRP2350A QFN-60 lifted from rp2350-bike-light, MT6701 from the sister project's encoder — both copied into hardware/parts/servodrive.kicad_sym so the projects open standalone.

Regenerate with python3 tools/gen_boards.py --force — which also stitches and fans out — and then python3 tools/route.py --rounds 4 --loose; --force throws the routing away, and without it the board is kept and only the drawings are redone. It refuses to write anything at all if the floorplan check fails.

Handed back to the sister project
  • Their F-17 is right — and draft 0.2 of this spec was wrong to doubt it. Their E-stop does brake, but not for the reason their register gives: R1, R2, R21 and R22 are 10 k pull-downs on both EG3113 inputs, and a pulled-down active-low LIN turns the low side on. The driver alone would coast. Worth telling them the consequence: their bridge hard-shorts the motor at every power-up, before firmware runs, with no current limit

2026-09-12
The electromagnetic simulation, ten phases. sim/ solves the board rather than estimating it: FastHenry on the copper as routed, FastCap for the sense taps, ngspice for the edges, openEMS full wave as a cross-check, plus the encoder field and the thermal path. Fourteen questions, twenty-four findings, four hypotheses — all four confirmed or resolved. Three answers change the design's own arithmetic: the commutation loop is 2.06 nH where geometry.commutation_loop() says 0.39, because VBUS reaches the high-side drain from In2 through a sixteen-via field rather than from a plane 0.1 mm away; twelve of those sixteen vias under each low-side drain are islands on B.Cu and carry nothing; and the DC-link ceramics are worth 5.5–9.7 µF of their 13.2 µF marking at 60 V. One number the board cannot settle decides several of the others — how hard the EG2103's output stage drives, which its only obtainable document gives without a test condition: 62.6 V of Vds and no snubber at the datasheet reading, 73.9 V and a 145 MHz ring at a strong output stage.

P7 was rebuilt, and the disagreement turned out to be the model. openEMS first returned 3.82 nH against FastHenry's 2.06 and it was recorded as a solver disagreement. It was five defects in the full-wave model: the conducting FETs were cylinders about an axis lying in F.Cu, which is 0.1525 mm above In1, so each rod cut about 1 mm² out of the ground plane and the high-side one shorted VBUS to GND at the drain; a port box sized from pad centres bit into the copper and none of its four coordinates survived the mesh merge; the excitation was a Gaussian centred at 750 MHz while the answer was read at 30–200 MHz; a band mean folded the loop's own 399 MHz resonance into “inductance”; and an unscaled ω² against a 1e8 intercept returned 1e36 nH. Fixed, the cross-check agrees to 8.5 % — 2.91 nH de-embedded against 2.68. There are no nets in FDTD, only metal: two conductors inside one cell are one conductor, and a mesh that looks converged can be converged on the wrong topology, which this one was twice on the way up.

Nothing in sim/ applies any of it. The hand-back list is fourteen proposed changes and six findings still open, all of them above.

2026-09-09
Board A routed, all of it. 1351 track segments, 283 vias, nothing unconnected, DRC and ERC clean, the heatsink land clear. The first routed board had made 69 of 170 connections and blamed the driver ring; measured, nearly every open connection ended at the RP2350, which had no room to fan out — a 0.4 mm-pitch QFN needs four staggered rows of vias, 2.9 mm deep, and the chip had 0.3 mm. The CPU wedge was rebuilt around that band, the FETs turned so their gates face the driver row instead of the heatsink land, the sense row rearranged for arcs, the shunts moved under the switch-node island so every 20 A pad takes a via, and the regulator cluster copied from Raspberry Pi's RP-006440 reference to the 0.05 mm — which found the bootstrap diode backwards, VREG_AVDD tied straight to 3V3, and no USB series resistors.

Then the router was measured rather than trusted, some of it by reading its bytecode. Freerouting joins a wire to a pad only if the wire ends on the pad's centre to the 0.1 µm; it cannot leave a fine-pitch pad that is not on the 45° grid; a protected wire is something it cannot tee into and, joined twice, something it normalises for ever; and its last pass can undo its earlier ones. So a new tools/fanout.py makes the copper it does badly — the QFN escape, the gate and sense arcs, the escapes, the plane taps — as locked tracks, and route.py hands the router vias instead of stubs and takes every finished net away from it.

That got to three open connections and stuck there, whichever router or order was used — which is the shape of a capacity limit, not of a search. It was: the escape vias sat in four rows 0.71 mm apart, and a 0.15 mm track passing radially between two rows needs 0.23 to clear a via pad, 0.15 of clearance and half its own width on each side, so 0.91 mm. There was no radial lane anywhere in the field. Pushing the outermost row alone out by 0.20 mm buys one the whole way round the chip for 0.2 mm of depth; with that, and a second try at the one pin the planned rows could not place, freerouting finished the board in its first round. Also new: tools/finish.py, a maze router over the board's rasterised free space, which closed connections when the board was still short of them and now has nothing to do.

2026-09-07
Phase cells in from 70° to 60°, using the channel interior to the screws. A cell is short of angle, not area, and angle costs radius: a millimetre of arc at R 20 is 2.9°, the same millimetre at R 29.5 is 1.9°. The cell was 70° because small parts sat at small radius — a 1 µF 0805 beside the gate driver took 30° on its own, while both MOSFETs together needed 18. The band the six boss pads sit on is empty between them, so the bootstrap diode, the DC-link 100 n, both input pulls and each phase's clamp diode moved out there at R 29.3, and the DC-link bulk to its own row at R 26.8.

Three consolidations paid for it: one 4.7 µF at the power link instead of a 1 µF per driver, the lead pad narrowed to ±7°, and the row packer taught to dodge parts in other rows. The result was 60/60 — which put the six bolts back on an even circle, the thing grouping the phases had cost.

Routing later took most of it back. A part in a phase cell's channel can only be connected if its nets are GND or the phase output: the phase-output pour owns B.Cu beneath it, the heatsink land owns F.Cu above it, and the bosses cut the outer ring into six arcs.
