Board E — Ethernet expansion

planned · placement sketched · not captured

A Ø65 mm daughter board that stacks on board S's expansion header, J13, and gives the drive 10/100 Ethernet: a WIZnet W5500 (hardwired TCP/IP, SPI) behind an RJ45 with its magnetics. Decided 2026-09-24; this page is the plan it will be captured and laid out from.

Decided

  • W5500, 10/100BASE-TX, one RJ45. The common RP2040/RP2350 route (WIZnet's own W5500-EVB-Pico). One port per drive: several axes need a switch. EtherCAT (LAN9252, two ports) and 10BASE-T1S multidrop (LAN8651) were the alternatives.
  • Ø65, stacked on J13, over board S's outward face.
  • The encoder has SPI0. Board S's encoder moved to hardware SPI0 on GPIO4/5/6 the same day, and the header's SPI pins, GPIO20–23, are the same SPI0. SPI1's clock exists only on GPIO10, 14 and 26 — phase B's PWM, port OUT's PIO UART and a current-sense ADC input. So the W5500 is driven by a PIO SPI on GPIO20–23. The RP2350 has three PIO blocks, and the RS-485 OUT port's UART uses part of one.

  • SPI: the W5500 takes mode 0 or 3 up to 80 MHz; a PIO SPI at a few tens of MHz leaves it far from the edge. GPIO20/21 are also I2C0, so an Ethernet board and an I2C device on the header exclude each other.
  • Power: the W5500 draws 132 mA transmitting at 100 Mbit/s (datasheet), the jack's two LEDs a few mA more: about 150 mA from +5V into an ME6211 LDO (0.26 W). Board S's +5V is its 1 A logic buck OR'd with USB, so the Ethernet board works on USB power alone. Its own 3.3 V spares board S's SPX3819, which already carries the logic.
  • Reachability: stacked, board E covers board S's BOOTSEL, test pads and SWD pads. Board E carries SWD and RUN pads and a reset button, and a hole over BOOTSEL.
  • Firmware: a PIO SPI (the pico-examples' pio/spi), INTn on GPIO19 as an interrupt, and WIZnet's ioLibrary (or lwIP with the W5500 in MACRAW). The FOC loop keeps hardware SPI0 for the encoder, undisturbed by network traffic.

Mechanics

  • Height. Board S's J13 is a 1.27 mm SMD male header; the only mating 2 × 10 sockets stocked are 4.3 mm tall (e.g. HC-PM127-4.3H-2x10PS, C27985222), which puts board E about 6 mm above board S. The bulk cans stand 9.5 mm (six Ø6.3 since 2026-09-28, two in each pocket between the M3 heads: R 13–17 at 37 and 66°, 123 and 150°, 311 and 326°), so board E has a hole for each, or one slot per pair. Clearing them outright would take a stacking height of about 11 mm and a different J13.
  • The XT30. Its plug goes in over board S's outward face for the first 5 mm, on the power wedge's axis (230°): board E needs a notch there, from the jack's face to the edge.
  • The RJ45 (HanRun HR911105A, 16 × 21 × 13.5 mm, magnetics and LEDs inside) goes radially at the rim over the CPU wedge, 256–308°: the power wedge has the XT30's plug path, the signal wedge the USB-C's mouth, and over the phase cells the heatsink ring clamps on the lands. Even there the 315° pocket's two cans (C1015 at R 10.6/311°, C1002 at R 16.9/326°) limit how far in a 21 mm jack reaches. The sketch below measured it: nowhere in 256–308° does it fit with its mouth on the edge; it clears the cans with the mouth 4.5 mm past the edge, at 283.5°.
  • Standoffs. A 1.27 mm header cannot take an RJ45's insertion force. Board E bolts to board S's M2.5 bosses on 6 mm standoffs, at least those at 256° and 308° either side of the jack.
  • The heatsink ring, if fitted, sits on the phase cells' lands under board E; its height is not decided and has to stay under board E's 6 mm.

Placement sketch

Board E over board S as routed: the cans, the XT30, BOOTSEL, the status LED and the six bosses are read from servodrive_S.kicad_pcb, and every courtyard is KiCad's own footprint's. Each part goes to the nearest legal spot to where it is wanted, legal meaning on the board, clear of every cutout and of every part on its face. All 40 parts are placed; nothing is captured or routed yet. python3 tools/placement_e.py redraws it.

  • The RJ45 needs a nose. At 21.3 mm deep, the HR911105A reaches back to the cans C1015 and C1002 wherever it stands in the CPU wedge. It clears them with its mouth 4.5 mm past the Ø65 edge, and then its LED pins are 6 mm behind the mouth, too close to the edge for copper. So board E grows a 121 mm² nose under the jack, out to its mouth. This is a decision, not a result. The other ways out: a shorter jack (magnetics separate), a vertical jack with the cable leaving along the motor axis, or a notch over only the XT30's plug path once its height is measured (that alone brings the overhang to about 3 mm).
  • The W5500 sits right behind the jack, its network pins toward the jack's pins and its SPI and crystal side toward the socket. The terminations and centre-tap caps sit between the two.
  • The socket is on the underside, contact for contact over J13; the sketch matches every pin by position. Everything else is on the top face, so board E is one SMD side plus the socket and the jack.
  • Cutouts: a hole or slot for each can (C1001 and C1012 share one); a notch over the XT30, its plug's path and the wires, kept whole because the XT30's and its plug's heights are not known; a 4 mm hole over BOOTSEL to press it with a pin; a window over the status LED; an M2.5 clearance hole over each of the six bosses.
  • Standoffs at H14 (256°) and H15 (308°) take the jack's insertion force; the other four holes give an optional third point. The jack's pins and pegs stand about 3 mm under board E, so they are kept clear of the standoffs' hex.
  • Board S's M3 heads stay under board E (3 mm against the 6 mm gap), so board E comes off before board S does.
  • Reset button opposite the jack; SWD and RUN pads beside the BOOTSEL hole.
